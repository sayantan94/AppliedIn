# Emphasis techniques (reference)

Detailed techniques for Step 2. Consult when a bullet or section is hard to
tailor without crossing into fabrication.

## Reordering
- Rank experiences and bullets by relevance to the JD's must-haves. Most
  relevant first, within each role and across the skills list.
- Drop nothing factual; demote the least relevant to the bottom.

## Vocabulary mirroring (truthful)
- If the seed says "message queues" and the JD says "event-driven / Kafka", and
  the seed's work WAS Kafka, use "Kafka" and "event-driven". If it was RabbitMQ,
  say RabbitMQ — mirror the concept, not a tool you didn't use.
- Match the JD's noun for a responsibility ("ownership", "on-call", "SLOs")
  only when the seed shows it.

## Quantification
- Surface numbers the seed already contains (throughput, latency, team size,
  $ impact). Never invent or inflate a number.
- If the seed has no number, keep the qualitative claim — do not fabricate one.

## Summary rewrite
- One or two sentences naming the role and the candidate's single strongest,
  true selling point for it. No adjectives the record doesn't earn.

## Skills section
- Reorder to put JD-named skills first. Only include skills present in the seed.
- Group by the JD's framing (e.g. "Languages / Infra / Data") if it helps scannability.

## When you're tempted to add something
You're not. If the JD wants a skill the candidate lacks, the honest move is to
emphasize the closest true experience — never to claim the missing skill.
